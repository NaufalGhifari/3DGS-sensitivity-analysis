Examining the Convergence and Hyperparameter Sensitivity on 3D Gaussian Splatting-Based Visual SLAM

Supplementary material for a study comparing rendering-quality and tracking-accuracy sensitivity across silhouette threshold, photometric loss weight, and isotropic regularization in SplaTAM and MonoGS, on Replica and TUM RGB-D scenes.

Muhammad Naufal Al Ghifari, Vektor Dewanto, Dina Chahyati
Faculty of Computer Science, Universitas Indonesia
Paper PDF — placeholder Code repository — placeholder BibTeX
Abstract

Summary

3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) has recently emerged as a fast, differentiable alternative to neural implicit representations for visual Simultaneous Localisation and Mapping (SLAM). SplaTAM and MonoGS are two prominent full-SLAM implementations based on 3DGS. However, both frameworks report some of their hyperparameters largely as fixed defaults, with limited documentation of sensitivity to variation. This technical report addresses this gap through a series of controlled experiments on the Replica Room0 dataset. We evaluate: (1) SplaTAM's tracking sensitivity to the silhouette threshold τ and its mapping sensitivity to photometric loss weighting λ, (2) MonoGS's rendering-tracking trade-off under isotropic regularisation and under a fully isotropic Gaussian shape constraint, and (3) residual gradient norm behaviour to assess whether both frameworks' fixed iteration budgets are sufficient for optimisation convergence. Results show SplaTAM's default τ = 0.99 is a well-justified operating point, with accuracy degrading sharply only near the extremes of its valid range, while its mapping stage is largely insensitive to λ with the assumption that a non-zero photometric term is applied. MonoGS's isotropic regularisation reveals a rendering-tracking trade-off pattern. Furthermore, forcing fully isotropic Gaussians (in MonoGS framework) degrades rendering quality without a corresponding tracking improvements. It is important to note that this is attributed to the absence of a dedicated compensatory mechanism in the ablation implementation rather than to the inherent limitation of isotropic properties. Gradient norm analysis indicates that tracking optimisation in both frameworks had not fully converged within their predefined iteration limits, and select MonoGS mapping parameters exhibited non-monotonic gradient behaviour in later iterations. The evidence hints that the fixed optimisation iteration number is based on heuristic-based computational budgets rather than convergence-derived criteria.

Summary

Key findings

Four observations recur across the sweeps below and organize the paper's central argument.

Rendering quality is stable; tracking accuracy is not

Across all three hyperparameter sweeps, PSNR follows a consistent, well-behaved curve for every scene which typically peaks at or near the baseline setting (0.99 - 0.9999). ATE RMSE, by contrast, has a scene-dependent optimum that shifts across hyperparameter values, sometimes disagreeing with the rendering-optimal setting entirely.

Synthetic scenes swing harder than real-world scenes

In the photometric loss weight experiment, synthetic Replica scenes (Office2, Room0) show an ~17–18 dB PSNR range across weights, while real world TUM scenes (fr1_desk, fr2_xyz) span only ~4–7 dB. This is consistent with real sensor noise, exposure variation, and motion blur compressing the dynamic range of the photometric signal. Although with only two scenes per domain, this remains a plausible explanation rather than statistically proven.

Isotropic regularization can flip direction within the same domain

Under MonoGS's isotropic regularization sweep, Office2's ATE improves monotonically with increasing λiso, while Room0's ATE worsens monotonically, despite both being synthetic Replica scenes rendered under identical conditions. Scene-dependence here is not fully explained by the synthetic-vs-real split alone. A further study focused on isotropic regularisation is warranted to better understand its sensitivity.

Anisotropic vs. isotropic Gaussians (MonoGS only)

Anisotropic Gaussians outperform isotropic ones on PSNR for all four scenes in MonoGS. This is expected: MonoGS's isotropic variant is a post-hoc retrofit (averaging the two in-plane scale parameters across all three axes), not a native design as in SplaTAM. This finding is scoped strictly to within-MonoGS settings.
